CHAPTER 93: SURAH AD-DUHA (سُورَةُ الضحى)

Classification: Makki

Order in Mushaf: 93

Total Verses: 11

Famous Names: Surat ad-Duha, Wad-Duha

1. Why This Surah Matters

Surah Ad-Duha is the tender, healing embrace of divine love for every broken, exhausted, and abandoned heart. Revealed after a agonizing silence of revelation (Inqita' al-Wahy) when the polytheists mocked the Prophet ﷺ saying: 'His Lord has abandoned him and hates him!', Allah swore by the radiant morning sunlight (Wad-Duha) and the peaceful, tranquil night (Wal-layli idha saja) to deliver the timeless reassurance: 'Your Lord has not forsaken you, nor is He displeased' (Ma wadda'aka Rabbuka wa ma qala). It promises that the future will be far better than the past (Wal-akhiratu khayrul-laka minal-ula), reminds him of his past rescues (orphanhood to shelter, lost to guided, poor to enriched), and commands loving service to orphans and seekers.

2. Surah Context

Revealed in Makkah after Gabriel did not descend for several days or weeks. The Prophet ﷺ was in deep emotional sorrow, fearing he had displeased his Lord, and Umm Jamil (the wife of Abu Lahab) taunted him. Surah Ad-Duha descended as pure heavenly consolation.

3. Central Theme

Divine Reassurance in Times of Emotional Darkness, the Promise of a Greater Future, Remembering Past Deliverances, and Ethical Treatment of the Vulnerable.

4. Key Passages

The Oaths of Light and Stillness (93:1–3)

$$\text{وَالضُّحَىٰ ۝ وَاللَّيْلِ إِذَا سَجَىٰ ۝ مَا وَدَّعَكَ رَبُّكَ وَمَا قَلَىٰ}$$

"By the morning brightness, And [by] the night when it covers with peace, Your Lord has not taken leave of you, [O Muhammad], nor has He detested [you]."

The Promise of Future Contentment (93:4–5)

$$\text{وَلَلْآخِرَةُ خَيْرٌ لَّكَ مِنَ الْأُولَىٰ ۝ وَلَسَوْفَ يُعْطِيكَ رَبُّكَ فَتَرْضَىٰ}$$

"And the Hereafter is better for you than the first [life]. And your Lord is going to give you, and you will be satisfied."

Remembering Past Grace and Serving Others (93:6–11)

$$\text{أَلَمْ يَجِدْكَ يَتِيمًا فَآوَىٰ ۝ وَوَجَدَكَ ضَالًّا فَهَدَىٰ ۝ وَوَجَدَكَ عَائِلًا فَأَغْنَىٰ ۝ فَأَمَّا الْيَتِيمَ فَلَا تَقْهَرْ ۝ وَأَمَّا السَّائِلَ فَلَا تَنْهَرْ ۝ وَأَمَّا بِنِعْمَةِ رَبِّكَ فَحَدِّثْ}$$

"Did He not find you an orphan and give [you] refuge? And He found you lost and guided [you], And He found you poor and made [you] self-sufficient. So as for the orphan, do not oppress [him]. And as for the petitioner, do not repel [him]. And as for the favor of your Lord, proclaim [it]."

5. Translation Highlights (The Clear Quran)

  • 93:3: 'Your Lord has not forsaken you, nor is He displeased.'
  • 93:5: 'And your Lord will give you, and you will be well-pleased.'
  • 93:11: 'And proclaim the blessings of your Lord.'

6. What Classical Tafsir Explains

  • The Pairing of Duha and Saja (Verses 1–2): Imam Ibn Kathir and classical mufassirun point out the psychological brilliance of these oaths: Allah pairs the radiant morning sun (Ad-Duha) with the peaceful, dark, still night (Al-Layli idha saja). Just as the sun cannot shine 24 hours without burning the earth, revelation and spiritual highs have natural cycles of quiet stillness. The pause in revelation was not abandonment; it was the peaceful night allowing the soul to rest before the next dawn.
  • The Promise of Eternal Contentment (Verse 5): Ibn Abbas (RA) stated: 'Part of the Prophet's ﷺ satisfaction is that not a single member of his Ummah who died upon monotheism will remain in the Hellfire!' The Prophet ﷺ said: 'Then by Allah, I will not be satisfied while one of my Ummah remains in the Fire!'

7. Important Supporting Hadiths

1. The Revelation of Surah Ad-Duha:

Sahih al-Bukhari (Hadith 4950) from Jundub ibn Abdillah (RA):

"The Prophet ﷺ fell ill and did not stand in night prayer for two or three nights. A woman (Umm Jamil) came and said: 'O Muhammad, I hope that your devil has abandoned you, for I have not seen him approach you for two or three nights!' Then Allah revealed: Wad-Duha, wal-layli idha saja, ma wadda'aka Rabbuka wa ma qala."

2. Takbir After Surah Ad-Duha:

Al-Mustadrak 'ala al-Sahihayn (Hadith 3848), Sahih:

"When Surah Ad-Duha was revealed, ending the painful silence, the Messenger of Allah ﷺ joyfully proclaimed: Allahu Akbar! It became a beloved Sunnah of recitation from Surah Ad-Duha to Surah An-Nas to pronounce the Takbir between surahs."

8. Key Concepts

1. Ma Wadda'aka Rabbuka (Divine Fidelity): The core assurance that periods of silence, emotional heaviness, or delays are never proof of God's abandonment.

2. Wa Lasawfa Yu'tika Rabbuka Fatarda (Guaranteed Contentment): The divine promise that future compensation and spiritual joy will completely erase past grief.

3. Tadhakkur an-Ni'am (Remembering Past Deliverances): Using memory of past rescues (from poverty, loneliness, and confusion) as fuel for current trust.

4. Hifz al-Yatim was-Sa'il (Protecting the Vulnerable): Translating divine gratitude into gentle care for orphans and empathetic patience with seekers.

9. Tadabbur (Guided Contemplation)

  • He Has Not Abandoned You (93:3): Whenever depression whispers, 'Allah doesn't care about you, your prayers are useless, you are all alone,' open Surah Ad-Duha. Read verse 3: Ma wadda'aka Rabbuka wa ma qala. Your Lord has not abandoned you, nor does He dislike you. The delay in your du'a is not rejection; it is the quiet night (Al-Layli idha saja) preparing your heart for the glorious dawn (Ad-Duha) of relief.
  • Look How Far He Has Brought You (93:6–8): When you are terrified about your future, look backward at your past: Alam yajidka yatiman fa-awa? Remember when you were emotionally broken, and He sheltered you? Remember when you were spiritually lost, and He guided you? Remember when you had zero dollars, and He fed you? The God who took care of you yesterday will not abandon you tomorrow.

10. What This Means For Me

  • In moments of sadness or spiritual burnout, I anchor my heart in Ma wadda'aka Rabbuka (My Lord has not abandoned me) (93:3).
  • I cultivate unshakable optimism, trusting that the future will be better than the past (Wal-akhiratu khayrul-laka minal-ula) (93:4).
  • I treat orphans, seekers, and service workers with gentle respect, never scolding or repelling them (93:9–10).
  • I speak about and share the blessings of Allah with gratitude (Wa amma bi-ni'mati Rabbika fahaddith) (93:11).

11. One Practical Action

Proclaim Three Blessings of Allah Today:

Fulfill verse 93:11 (Wa amma bi-ni'mati Rabbika fahaddith): In conversation with your family or friends today, explicitly mention three specific blessings Allah has granted you recently, giving praise to Him.

12. Reflection Questions

1. Do I mistake periods of silence or delayed prayers for divine abandonment (93:3)?

2. How do my past rescues by Allah (from confusion, lack, or loneliness) give me confidence for my future (93:6–8)?

3. Am I treating vulnerable people and those asking for help with gentleness, or with irritation (93:9–10)?

13. Closing Supplication

$$\text{اللَّهُمَّ يَا رَبَّ الضُّحَىٰ وَاللَّيْلِ إِذَا سَجَىٰ ، لَا تَدَعْنَا وَلَا تَقْلِنَا ، اللَّهُمَّ أَعْطِنَا حَتَّىٰ نَرْضَىٰ ، وَاجْعَلْ آخِرَتَنَا خَيْرًا لَنَا مِنَ الْأُولَىٰ ، وَارْزُقْنَا رِعَايَةَ الْيَتِيمِ وَإِكْرَامَ السَّائِلِ}$$

"O Allah, Lord of the morning brightness and the night when it covers in peace: Do not take leave of us and do not detest us. O Allah, give us until we are satisfied, make our Hereafter better for us than our first life, and bless us to care for the orphan and honor the seeker. Amin."
